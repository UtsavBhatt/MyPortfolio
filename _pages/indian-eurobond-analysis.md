# Indian Eurobond Market Research

### Market Structure, Pricing, Risk Management and the Indian Corporate Perspective

**Project type:** Academic Research & Applied Financial Analysis

**Domain:** Fixed Income | Corporate Debt | International Capital Markets

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project examines Eurobonds as an international financing instrument and evaluates their economics from the perspective of a fixed income investor.

The study combines an understanding of international debt market structures with bond valuation, yield analysis, credit spreads and foreign exchange risk.

The applied component examines Reliance Industries Limited's January 2022 US-dollar bond issuance, using its three-tranche transaction as a practical case study for understanding bond investment decisions.

## 2. Research Objectives

The project addresses the following questions:

- How are Eurobonds structured, issued and distributed in international capital markets?
- How do benchmark interest rates and credit spreads influence offshore bond pricing?
- How should an investor evaluate a bond beyond its headline coupon?
- How do duration, yield, NPV and IRR influence investment decisions?
- How does foreign exchange exposure affect the economics of international debt?
- What should an investor examine before purchasing and monitoring a corporate Eurobond?

## 4. Applied Case Study: Reliance Industries Limited

The applied analysis examines RIL's January 2022 US$4 billion senior unsecured bond issuance.

| Tranche | Issue size | Maturity | Coupon |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2032 | US$1.50bn | 2032 | 2.875% |
| 2052 | US$1.75bn | 2052 | 3.625% |
| 2062 | US$0.75bn | 2062 | 3.750% |

The transaction provides a practical context for evaluating different maturities, yields, benchmark rates and investor return requirements.

The analysis examines the investment decision from the perspective of bond pricing, required returns, interest rate exposure and currency considerations.

## 5. Key Analytical Takeaways

The project highlights several important principles:

**Coupon is not investment return.**

An investor must work backwards from the market price to understand the yield actually available.

**Benchmark rates and credit spreads must be evaluated separately.**

A bond's yield reflects both the underlying interest rate environment and issuer-specific credit compensation.

**Duration matters.**

Longer-maturity bonds can carry substantial sensitivity to changes in interest rates.

**Currency risk changes the economics of offshore debt.**

For an investor or issuer exposed to INR/USD movements, the exchange rate can materially affect the domestic-currency value of cash flows.

**Bond evaluation requires a complete framework.**

Coupon, price, yield, spread, credit quality, duration and currency exposure should be considered together.
